# 5. Beauty → Meaning → Ritual, and Fear

← [Back to Talk 14 README](README.md)

> Quotations are translated from the Vietnamese transcript. Original wording: [05_su-dep-nghia-le-su-so_vi.md](05_su-dep-nghia-le-su-so_vi.md)

> This is the talk's original idea — not a history summary but a model Vũ built himself. It is also the most heavily challenged part, and it is unfinished. Vũ said at the end: *"I still don't really understand this idea, it's just the best way I can express it right now."*
>
> This file records the model **together with** the objections, because the objections are the most valuable part.

**On the terms.** The Vietnamese words are **sự đẹp** (Beauty), **nghĩa** (Meaning), **lễ** (Ritual) and **sự sợ** (Fear). The *lễ / nghĩa* pair is the Vietnamese form of the Confucian *lǐ* 禮 / *yì* 義 pairing — *lễ* is rite, propriety, the performed form; *nghĩa* is the rightness or substance the form is supposed to carry. "Ritual / Meaning" is the closest English rendering but loses that resonance.

---

## The model

```
   chaos, nothing left to trust
              ↓
         BEAUTY   (quality — internal, everyone has it)
              ↓
         MEANING  (the significance behind an action — context-dependent, hard to transmit)
              ↓
         RITUAL   (action, rite, formula — easy to copy, context-independent)
              ↓
          FEAR    (what keeps Ritual alive after Meaning is gone)
```

---

## Each layer

### Beauty

It appears when there is nothing left to lean on:

> *"When everything is extremely chaotic and there is nothing at all, then usually the people who take the first steps to [build] something are driven by a thing called beauty."*

Contrast with the normal state, in which we **do not need** beauty:

> *"Today we have a logical system and it works pretty good. We can trust the stock market system, the government system, the military system, the logic system... so we don't have to think too hard about what is right and what is wrong — we simply continue the old belief."*

And the reverse scenario:

> *"Chaotic periods — say in about 50 years, or 100 years, or 20 years — everything gets so chaotic that nothing can be trusted any more, and then suddenly we have what's called a crisis of belief."*

Two things Vũ attaches to Beauty: **courage**, and **being forced to be very honest with yourself**.

Asked directly what beauty is, the clearest answer he gave:

> *"It's something internal, everyone has it. It's the thing that makes me move forward."*

Two examples he used to point at it:
- **Writing poetry.** *"Poetry is extremely hard to make if you count it mathematically — we have billions of options. But somehow we can pick up a very short passage of verse. Where did that verse come from?"*
- **Poincaré.** The mathematician tried two methods: sitting 10 hours a day wringing his brain, or walking and riding the bus. *"He realized the bus was more effective for solving mathematics."*

And the reasoning behind both examples:

> *"We use logic to make decisions, and we produce a thing called a hypothesis, and we look for a way to prove that hypothesis. But there's a question: where did the hypothesis come from?"*

This is the strongest argument for something existing prior to logic. Logic tests hypotheses; it does not generate them. (A participant named it immediately: *intuition*. Vũ neither rejected nor accepted it, since intuition is also just another name.)

### Meaning

Meaning is the significance behind an action — and it has two decisive properties:

1. **Very hard to transmit.** *"This meaning is very hard to convey, and across a few generations it usually disappears."*
2. **It changes with context.** And this is where it differs from Ritual fundamentally.

Vũ's example — **courage**:

> *"What is courage? Sometimes running is courage, sometimes charging onto the battlefield is courage, sometimes [hiding behind] your mother's skirt is courage. It cannot be defined."*

And a second — **fighting for freedom**:

> *"Today we still fight for freedom, but we can see that our fighting for freedom today, compared to the old one, has something that isn't quite right. Even though we can use the old logic, the old sayings — applied to the present circumstance, something isn't right, and we can't explain it."*

That *"can't explain it"* is the important diagnosis: we can feel that Meaning has drifted, but cannot point to it in words. Precisely because Meaning is not a verbal thing.

### Ritual

Ritual is the action layer — rite, formula, concrete definition. *"It is the actions that we perform."* Vũ used Vietnam as the example of a country with a great deal of *lễ nghĩa*.

Ritual has the two inverse properties:

1. **Easily copied.** *"Meaning is very hard to copy, but ritual we can copy very easily."*
2. **Does not change with context.** *"When the context changes we have to change what we do — but ritual doesn't; with ritual we will do exactly the same thing."*

And what happens when someone says the Ritual has gone wrong?

> *"If someone says you're wrong, what do we do? We'll use dialectic or rhetoric, or whatever tool we have, to argue back at them on social media."*

Here the model connects straight back to [file 02](02_rhetoric-and-dialectic.md): rhetoric and dialectic get conscripted to **defend the Ritual**, not to recover the Meaning.

### Fear

This is the piece Vũ added to the model this time (earlier sessions had only beauty). The mechanism:

> *"We gradually lose the idea of what is happening. But because we don't want to admit that we don't know what is happening, the easiest way, the safest way, and the way that also feels most effective at the time, is simply to copy whatever has already been accepted."*

So Fear is not fear of a specific object. It is **fear of having to admit you don't understand**. And its product is copying Ritual.

Vũ also described Fear at the individual level, where it gets more concrete:

> *"Fear is the thing hidden right behind, and it's usually masked by things like emotion, feeling, or even sometimes logic."*

Example: we choose B rather than A, then explain to ourselves "this was the rational choice." But *"if we sit deep enough and sit long enough, the real reason we chose decision B becomes much clearer — and we'll see our own fears pushing us toward B, to protect some image of ourselves."*

**The nuclear fear:** protecting your own image. And it links to **status**:

> *"A human being seeks status a great deal — even if we deny that we seek status, we do seek status. Status isn't only defined as social position; it can be social recognition."*

Vũ applied it to himself, and this is the most honest point in the talk:

> *"This talk today has a part that is me wanting to share this knowledge, and also some part of it that is wanting to prove, to defend some image of myself. And it has both the beauty part and the fear part in its motive."*

---

## The social cycle

Vũ sketched a curve:

> *"From a very bad point it develops gradually upward, then it reaches a peak — for instance defeating a very large enemy. Then once it has reached that peak it will hold, meaning it will decline over time, as we keep copying that ritual but things are still more or less fine — until that ritual accumulates enough of society's bad parts, and then usually society has to go [boom] and it collapses immediately."*

The historical case he mapped onto it: Greece was built out of the **Dark Age** — a dark period born from the collapse of global trade, extreme wealth disparity, plus natural disasters. *"We could no longer use the old logic"* → so they produced **eudaimonia**. Beauty produced Meaning.

And on Athens: *"the same idea"* both lifted Greece up and *"slaughtered a great many people once Athens began to degenerate."* The idea did not change — only Meaning withdrew from it.

> A point Vũ pressed under questioning: *"those ideas really are neither good nor bad."* What matters is not the content of the idea but **the road by which we arrived at it**.

---

## Worked example: hypergamy

Vũ used marrying up (hypergamy) as a test case.

**The original Meaning was real.** In the past, *"women had almost no choice in education and almost no freedom at all, so hypergamy actually makes a fair amount of sense — because it was more or less the only way they could [rise] in society."*

**The context has changed.** Vũ offered a datum and paused to ask the room who was surprised: *"in the overwhelming majority of countries, women now have the advantage in education over men"* — exceptions he named being Japan, some Middle Eastern countries, parts of Africa. Plus *"we are also in an economy where knowledge is the thing that gets paid more."*

**But the Ritual still runs.** *"It was a best practice back then, but we'll keep it right up to today."*

**And Fear is what keeps it.** *"Oh, the person next to me also married up — why should I sacrifice?"*

This is the model's best illustration, because it shows all four layers without needing any history.

## The objections — the most important part

Nhật pushed back continuously and did not yield. Below are four lines of attack, with how far Vũ got in answering.

### Line 1: Is Fear good or bad? You're contradicting yourself.

> *"I don't understand whether fear, to you, is something necessary or something unnecessary... we're seeing it self-conflict with Vũ's presentation from the beginning."*

This is a very precise catch. In the first part of the talk, Fear is clearly what makes society decay (copying Ritual without understanding Meaning). In the later part, Vũ says Fear is a **signal** to observe, and observing it is good.

Vũ could not fully extricate himself. He retreated to: *"I'll just stop at observing it; the decision is a personal decision."* And asked directly whether fear should be minimized as much as possible: *"I wouldn't go that far... I haven't gone so far as whether it's good or bad."*

**Status:** unresolved. The model needs to separate *Fear as mechanism* from *Fear as diagnostic signal*.

### Line 2: Meditation is just how you justify yourself.

> *"I think instead that you meditate in order to justify a decision. And that's also just a way for you to autosuggest to yourself that this is a good decision."*

Vũ admitted it outright: *"that could happen."* And defended only by describing the experience: at the retreat, *"in Tokyo there are so many things pushing into your brain, but the retreat doesn't have those"*, and *"there were moments when I tried to think about that worry but the worry couldn't come up."*

Importantly, he clarified that meditation does **not** supply the answer. When Nhật paraphrased it as "so you're more satisfied with your decision," Vũ corrected: *"I haven't decided... I realize there are other hidden options."* The effect is to **widen the option set**, not to choose.

**Status:** the objection stands. There is no external way to distinguish "seeing more clearly" from "persuading yourself more thoroughly." Vũ was right not to pretend there is.

### Line 3: This is a personal journey, not a social law.

The most persistent line, repeated through the end of the talk:

> *"I don't accept the beauty and fear you just described — I think it is only at the individual level. It is the individual level. And as for this idea rising or falling like this, I haven't seen that. I don't regard it as a meaningful graph, because I hold that all these stories simply happen and have their own meaning — they don't rise and fall the way we imagine an idea gradually sinking."*

And she offered her own version, which is in fact quite compact:

> *"When we go to understand some story, some theory, it always has a meaning behind it. At first we won't understand — like the cooking thing — if we don't quite understand we can follow the recipe. After we've been brought to understand, we'll have our own recipe. And the journey from fear to beauty is just a personal journey like that — and it will be everyone's business, not something to do with an idea [rising or falling]."*

Note: she **uses Vũ's own cooking analogy** against Vũ's conclusion. That is a good move, and it shows that [file 06](06_recipes-and-wisdom.md) is the common ground both can stand on.

Vũ narrowed twice:
1. Redefined the "decline" axis: *"the decline here is its strength, its level of influence — not that the decline is right or wrong."*
2. Fully affirmed the individual layer: *"I reaffirm completely that it is individual — but the social circumstance makes these individuals sometimes have very similar reactions."*

And offered a final defensive example: *"a few hundred years ago in Europe we believed in God and didn't believe in money, but today everything believes in money. Whether you believe in God or believe in money isn't the issue — the issue is why we believe in it."*

**Status:** a substantive concession. The post-argument version is weaker than the original, but **sturdier**. See [file 10](10_individual-or-society.md) for the full exchange.

### Line 4: You're afraid of something and therefore calling it bad.

> *"When Vũ brings his own emotion in to evaluate events or theories outside himself... it becomes a slightly skewed view, because Vũ is afraid of something and Vũ holds that the thing is bad."*

And the compressed version, which she named as the one thing she wanted to say: *"when we're afraid of something, it doesn't mean the other thing is bad."*

Vũ agreed — and his answer here is the best part of Part 3:

> *"The nature of the action literally doesn't really matter. [Take] looking for a high-paying job. There will be some people who have to overcome a great deal, who have to overcome many fears to do it. Whereas for me, the same decision might come from fear — fear of falling behind, or keeping up an image. So the same action, two different people: one may come from beauty, one may come from fear. And the action is identical."*

The methodological conclusion follows:

> *"If we're arguing at the ritual layer we can never go deeper. We have to [ask] why we are doing this — and that will be an individual question."*

**Status:** this is where the model genuinely does work. It does not judge actions; it asks about motive, and only at the individual level.

### And a closing blow

Nhật ended with a line that got the room laughing:

> *"When you talk about beauty, I'm certain that in argument you will never beat my doctrine of blandness."*

Vũ: *"maybe."*

She stated her position plainly: *"in my doctrine of blandness there is beauty too, but I don't regard the pursuit of beauty as inevitable."*

That is a real position, not just a joke: **beauty exists, but pursuing it is not obligatory.** Vũ's whole model assumes the pursuit is inevitable — so if you drop that assumption, the model has no footing.

### Thái: a diagnosis about the pattern

Thái did not argue the content; he argued **how the idea was generated** — and this is the hardest objection to dislodge:

> *"Every time he goes to the retreat he observes some moment inside his own mind, and sums it up into an idea, and projects that idea into a social ideal. This is a pattern."*

And the closing image:

> *"Vũ's idea is just one node in a neural network. It's just one puzzle piece in one circumstance — and Vũ has already concluded it's the whole picture."*

Vũ answered: *"I understand."*

And then admitted something important about his own method:

> *"I have had an idea [pointing] in some direction from the very start, and I'm trying to find the ladder — enough evidence and citations — to reach that direction."*

Or, as someone else rephrased it and Vũ agreed: *"you could say I'm looking for a road to interpret it so people understand."*

This is an admission that he is doing **rhetoric**, not **dialectic** — by exactly the definitions he gave at the start of the talk (see [file 02](02_rhetoric-and-dialectic.md)). Conclusion first, argument after. Saying so out loud is to his credit, but it does not neutralize the objection.

## Current state of the idea

**What holds up:**
- The Meaning / Ritual distinction, with a clear criterion: Meaning is context-dependent and hard to transmit; Ritual is context-independent and easy to copy. This distinction does work.
- The mechanism: the same action can come from two different motives — so arguing at the level of the action is futile.
- The argument that logic does not generate hypotheses, so where do hypotheses come from.
- The diagnostic: when you are defending something with logic and feel something is off, that is a sign Meaning has withdrawn from Ritual.

**What does not hold up yet:**
- "Beauty" has no definition. It is carrying too much: quality, beauty, eudaimonia, intuition, courage, forward motivation.
- "Is Fear good or bad" is unresolved.
- The social-cycle model (rise–peak–drift–collapse) has been reduced to "level of influence" and still has no way to be tested.
- The jump from introspection to social law is unjustified — Thái's diagnosis, and it was not refuted.

**What to do next:**
1. Define Beauty by what it is **not**, rather than by synonyms.
2. Split Fear into two separate concepts (mechanism vs diagnostic signal) to clear the self-contradiction.
3. Either drop the social layer entirely, or find a test criterion for it. It is currently the weakest part and the most attacked.
4. Read Nietzsche. Both Vũ and Nhật noticed the overlap during the talk — *"welcome to Nietzsche's thinking"* — and Vũ agreed: *"it's fairly similar, because Nietzsche and the Greeks... they all saw this part, it's just that their interpretations are very different."* The repo already has [Nietzsche/Analysis](../../Nietzsche/Analysis/analysis.md).
