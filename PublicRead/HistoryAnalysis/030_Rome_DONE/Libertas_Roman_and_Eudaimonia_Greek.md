# Libertas (Roman) vs Eudaimonia (Greek)

| Concept | Libertas (Rome) | Eudaimonia (Greece) |
|----------|----------------|-------------------|
| Core Meaning | Freedom, liberty, independence | Human flourishing, fulfillment, living well |
| Main Question | "Am I free?" | "Am I living a good life?" |
| Focus | Political and civic status | Ethical and personal excellence |
| Domain | Law, citizenship, politics | Virtue, character, philosophy |
| Opposite | Slavery, domination, tyranny | Misery, vice, failure to flourish |

## Libertas (Roman)

**Libertas** refers to freedom from arbitrary domination and the status of being a free citizen.

Key elements:
- Freedom under the law
- Protection from tyranny
- Political participation
- Civic independence
- Republican self-government

For Romans, liberty was primarily a **political and legal condition**. A person was free when they were not subject to the arbitrary will of another.

---

## Eudaimonia (Greek)

**Eudaimonia** is often translated as:
- Human flourishing
- Fulfillment
- Living well
- The highest human good

Eudaimonia is achieved through:
- Virtue (*aretē*)
- Rational activity
- Moral excellence
- A complete life well-lived

It is not simply pleasure or emotional happiness. Rather, it is the realization of human potential through virtuous living.
